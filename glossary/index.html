Register · Lexicon

Lexicon

55 terms in the setting’s vocabulary — from the mechanism of the game to the names the Neu Republik gave its monsters.

55 shown
01

Active

Engine

The normal operating state of a Rift die. The technology works as intended: no bonus, no penalty.

See also: Rift Dice

02

Armoured Walker Platoon

Engine

The new platoon type: one to three walkers, optionally with infantry, a vehicle and an anti-tank team. Lets a commander field a single walker with no command upgrade — just a large piece of Rift-tech walking down the table.

See also: Assault Platoon · Walker

03

Assault Platoon

Engine

The core force-selection building block of Konflikt ’47, in place of Bolt Action’s Rifle Platoon. Command, specialist and advanced infantry, with support options.

See also: Armoured Walker Platoon

04

Automated Infantry

Unit

Commonwealth combat automatons. March in formation, take casualties without loss of cohesion, and require no letters home. Described by British officers as equipment, and by British soldiers as something else.

See also: MkIIC Automated Director · B for Bertie

05

Bolt Action

Engine

Warlord’s World War II game, and Konflikt ’47’s mechanical ancestor. Order Dice and Pins are inherited directly; the Rift is what makes this a different game.

See also: Order Dice · Pin

06

Cerberus Squad

Unit

Commonwealth Rift-enhanced attack dogs. The programme is real, unpublicised, and its handlers do not discuss it.

See also: British Commonwealth

07

Compression weapon

Technology

The Japanese "Shiboru" weapons: devices that fold a target in on itself. The mechanism is unexplained in the published lore, which is consistent with how Japan’s programme is presented.

See also: Empire of Japan

08

Daughters of the Motherland

Unit

Elite enhanced Soviet infantry, and the most carefully managed propaganda asset in the war. Real soldiers, deployed where the cameras and the casualty estimates agree.

See also: Soviet Bloc

09

Dresden Defiant

Campaign

The closing chapter of Festung Europa: the Rift city itself, and the three alliances bleeding for an objective none of them can take.

See also: Dresden Rift

10

Dresden Rift

Rift

The second Rift, opened in the crater of the atomic bombing of Dresden in March 1944. The strategic objective of the entire European war; nobody holds it in a way that counts.

See also: Dresden · Rift

11

Exhausted

Engine

A spent Rift die. The unit pays for its earlier brilliance with a penalty — slowed, reduced output, or simply less dangerous. In the fiction this is the price of touching something that was not built to be touched.

See also: Rift Dice

12

Festung Europa

Campaign

The first supplement of the Rift War series: 116 pages covering the European theatre from February 1944 to February 1947, three operations, and the Dresden campaign.

See also: Operation Pluto · Operation Touchdown

13

Fireflies

Unit

United States jump infantry, the spearhead of American doctrine. Arrive vertically, fight at close range, and are expected to keep moving.

See also: Roberta Wells

14

Galahad

Unit

British powered armour: cheaper and more flexible than Stahltruppen, and repairable in quantities that matter.

See also: Stahltruppen

15

Ghost Infantry

Unit

Japanese phasing assault infantry who step briefly out of the physical world. Their own name for the phase realm is "Shinobi Space".

See also: Shinobi Space

16

Green Vault

Power

A conspiracy that steered European history from the shadows for centuries, used the Nazi regime as an instrument, and stepped into the open when the Dresden Rift gave it the power to rule directly.

See also: Neu Republik

17

Guts

Engine

A hero-only stat for stubbornness: modifying a die after it is rolled, buying an extra activation, or refusing to be removed as a casualty.

See also: Rift Mastery · Luck

18

Invasion Amerika

Campaign

The second Rift War volume, shifting the narrative to the Pacific and to Japan’s ambitions against the American continent.

See also: Empire of Japan

19

Los Alamos

Rift

The New Mexico test site where Trinity opened the first Rift on 6 February 1944. Now a shrine with a laboratory attached, and the centre of the American Paragon programme.

See also: Paragon Programme

20

Luck

Engine

A hero-only stat representing the setting’s fondness for the undeserved survival of named characters.

See also: Guts

21

MkIIC Automated Director

Unit

A command automaton coordinating Commonwealth automated formations. Whether it thinks is a question the War Office avoids answering in public.

See also: Automated Infantry

22

Moroz Gul

Unit

Soviet terror troops hardened against cold and against horror. Deployed where a line has already broken.

See also: Ursus

23

Nachtalben

Unit

The serum variant that resembles folklore’s vampire closely enough that Allied reports read like superstition. Upright, capable of speech, and comfortable in darkness.

See also: Schreckwulfen

24

Nachtjäger

Unit

The further-pushed strain of the Dresden serum programme. Less recognisably human than the Schreckwulfen, deployed at night, and rarely photographed.

See also: Schreckwulfen · Nachtalben

25

Necromobilization

Rift

The programme and the policy of turning the dead into military supply. The subject of an Allied conference in late 1946 that tried, and failed, to establish what the reanimated legally are.

See also: Totenkorps · Conference on Necromobilization

26

Neu Republik

Power

The state the Green Vault founded after purging the Nazi leadership. Authoritarian, competent, and built around the Dresden Rift. Its ministers speak of the American attack as an inheritance rather than a crime.

See also: Green Vault · Dresden Rift

27

Onryo

Unit

A vengeful spirit in the Imperial order of battle, named from Japanese ghost tradition. Present, documented, and not comfortably explained.

See also: Empire of Japan

28

Operation Pluto

Campaign

Allied offensive, 12 February to 14 June 1947. The first test of whether the Neu Republik’s line can still be moved by conventional mass. It cannot.

See also: Festung Europa

29

Operation Touchdown

Campaign

The deepest Allied thrust into Germany, 23 June to 6 September 1947, with Roberta Wells and the 109th Airborne Division leading.

See also: Roberta Wells

30

Operation Valentine

Campaign

Two-phase Allied offensive in 1947, 14 February to 10 July and 30 July to 10 October, aimed at the approaches to the Dresden Rift.

See also: Festung Europa

31

Order Dice

Engine

Inherited from Bolt Action: one die per unit, drawn blind from a shared bag, each face an order — Fire, Advance, Run, Ambush, Rally, Down. The engine of the game’s fog of war.

See also: Pin

32

Pan-Atlantic Alliance

Power

The wartime partnership of the United States, Britain, the Commonwealth and the surviving European governments. Held together by shared enemies and strained by American refusal to share Rift technology.

See also: United States · British Commonwealth

33

Paragon Programme

Unit

The American supersoldier project, run out of Los Alamos. Genetically enhanced, reportedly psychic, and officially not discussed.

See also: Los Alamos

34

Pin

Engine

A marker for a unit under fire. Pins make orders harder to obey. In Konflikt ’47 far more units ignore them, which is why the shooting is so much more lethal than its parent game.

See also: Order Dice

35

Rift Dice

Engine

The signature mechanical device of the game. Every Rift unit contributes a die that exists in one of three states, drawn from the same bag as Order Dice and assigned to Rift units. Its state decides how well the technology behaves.

See also: Exhausted · Active · Surging

36

Rift Energy

Rift

The physical residue of an open Rift. All Rift systems draw on it, none fully understand it, and every faction measures its science by how much of it they can hold steady.

See also: Rift Dice

37

Rift Mastery

Engine

A hero-only stat allowing a character to hold and spend an additional Rift die, to feed dice to nearby Rift units, or to combine two Active dice into a Surging one. Heroes are, in mechanical terms, people who can negotiate with the Rift.

See also: Guts · Luck

38

Rift War series

Campaign

Warlord’s narrative supplement line. Volume one is Festung Europa; volume two, Invasion Amerika, turns to the Pacific; a third volume was on pre-order in late 2026.

See also: Invasion Amerika

39

Rift-tech

Rift

The applied science derived from Rift study. Not a single technology but a family of them: gravity weapons, Tesla arcs, sonic projectors, walker locomotion, serum biology and necromancy.

See also: Rift · Rift Dice

40

Schienenkanone

Technology

The Axis hyper-velocity rail gun, capable of tearing through the thickest armour. Mounted on walkers and retrofitted tank hulls such as the Panther X.

See also: Rift-tech · Panther X

41

Schreckwulfen

Unit

The first successful Axis supersoldier: serum-enhanced, bestial, and committed to the front in 1945 where they broke Allied infantry formations badly enough to trigger every rival programme.

See also: Nachtjäger · Nachtalben

42

Schwerefeld

Technology

Axis gravity weapons. A Schwerefeld projector crushes flesh and steel without needing to defeat armour first, which makes conventional protection close to irrelevant.

See also: Rift-tech · Thor

43

Science Cities

Power

The Soviet research complex that produced the Ursus, the Moroz Gul and the Daughters of the Motherland. The phrase appears in Soviet in-fiction quotes and carries a great deal of implication about who the subjects were.

See also: Ursus · Moroz Gul

44

Shibito

Unit

Japanese undead infantry: fearless, obedient, and driven to fight until the body fails. The Imperial answer to attrition, industrialised after the atomic strikes.

See also: Totenkorps

45

Shinobi Space

Rift

The Japanese term for the phase realm entered by Ghost units. Entered briefly, at cost, and returned from changed.

See also: Ghost Infantry

46

Soviet Bloc

Power

The Soviet Union and its satellites. No Rift of their own; a policy of capture, reverse-engineering and human experimentation instead. Named for the bloc, not the union, because the setting treats it as a single war machine.

See also: Science Cities

47

Stahltruppen

Unit

Axis powered infantry, sealed in plate that defeats small arms. The image Allied soldiers carry of the Neu Republik: an unhurried metal figure walking through everything they have.

See also: Galahads · Assault Frame

48

Surging

Engine

An overloaded Rift die. Where the setting’s worst and best moments happen: Tesla arcs that ignore cover, walkers that move like infantry, soldiers who phase. Surging is always temporary and always paid for.

See also: Rift Dice

49

Tesla weapon

Technology

Electrical arc weaponry fielded by the United States and the Commonwealth. At full Rift output it gains shots, accuracy against vehicles, and the ability to ignore cover.

See also: Rift Dice · M4A9-T Sherman

50

The Rift

Rift

A tear in space and time, opened by atomic detonation. Two are known to exist: one at Los Alamos, one at Dresden. Everything else in this setting — the technology, the biology, the dead who keep walking — is something that came through or was learned by looking at one.

See also: Rift-tech · Dresden Rift · Los Alamos

51

Totenkorps

Unit

The Axis undead infantry arm. Not resurrected individuals but an industrial process: casualties collected, injected, uniformed and returned to the line. Immune to pain and fear; capable of rising again after being put down.

See also: Necromobilization · Totenrotte

52

Totenrotte

Unit

A Totenkorps formation under a Vault-vetted commander. The commander is the shepherd; the dead are the flock; the distinction matters tactically.

See also: Totenkorps

53

Ursus

Unit

Soviet heavy infantry built from human and bear material, eight feet tall, designed specifically to survive contact with a Schreckwulfen. Some carry mauls.

See also: Moroz Gul · Science Cities

54

Walker

Rift

Legged armoured fighting vehicle. Mass-produced, not magical: a walker can be destroyed by infantry anti-tank teams, ordinary tanks and guns. The fascination is the form, not invulnerability.

See also: Armoured Walker Platoon

55

Zvukovoy

Technology

Soviet sonic weaponry, using harmonic resonance to shake a target apart rather than penetrate it. Most commonly mounted on armoured chassis such as the T-34/ZP.

See also: Rift-tech
