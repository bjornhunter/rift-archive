A war of monsters and machines is still decided by people with names. These are the ones the record has named — and the ones whose names are attached to the decisions rather than the fighting.

I · Battlefield personnel

United States

Roberta Wells

active

Rift researcher · Firefly

The United States’ foremost authority on Rift energy, and therefore the person most exposed to what the Axis has been doing with it. Wells has spent the war investigating Rift Horrors and the Dresden wound; in 1947 she is attached to the 109th Airborne Division and flying into Germany in prototype Firefly armour. Warlord describes her as the best hope America has of assessing the Dresden Rift — and of neutralising it.

Released with Festung Europa as an alternative sculpt in experimental armour, and separately as a stand-alone hero.

Axis

Oberstarzt Albrecht von Zögling

a.k.a. Der Erntemann · The Harvestman

active

Director, Totenkorps programme

The man who made the dead a resource. Von Zögling runs the Totenkorps programme from inside the Dresden apparatus, and his nickname became literal after the bombs fell and he found he could summon the dead back in numbers that bordered on the infinite. He leads from the front in a sealed suit, carrying medical equipment, looking for new experimental material. He is the closest thing the setting has to a villain with a laboratory and a rank.

The Axis counterpart to Wells in the Festung Europa launch set.

Axis

Hauptmann Heinrich Gross

active

Infantry officer · re-educated

A German officer singled out during the Green Vault takeover for "special training and re-education", and returned to the line as something more useful to the Neu Republik than the man who went in. Gross is the pattern the Vault prefers: competence that has been corrected. Allied intelligence rates him as a conventional officer with an unconventional willingness to spend his own men.

One of the earliest Axis character releases in the current edition.

Commonwealth

B for Bertie

unknown

Anomalous MkI automaton

A battle-damaged MkI automaton that was written off and then observed — repeatedly, and across a wide stretch of the Western Front — assisting Allied forces without orders, programme or handler. The Commonwealth keeps him on the books and does not put him in the manuals. How much of Bertie is intelligence and how much is a very complicated set of failures is a question nobody at the War Office wants answered in writing.

The setting’s one piece of genuinely unexplained kindness.

Japan

Kimura Tadaaki

active

Imperial officer · hero

Kimura is the named face of the Imperial Japanese character range — an officer in a faction that is otherwise presented as a collective will. His release coincides with Japan’s promotion from a supporting power to a leading one in the Rift War narrative, with the second volume, Invasion Amerika, turning the war toward the Pacific.

Released in the first wave of Japanese hero miniatures.

Soviet Bloc

Molot

active

Hero · Soviet Bloc

The Soviet Bloc’s first named battlefield character in the current edition, released alongside the Ursus plastics. Warlord has kept his background deliberately thin so far: a hero of the Union, generated by the Science Cities, and presented as evidence that the Bloc’s improvised Rift science can produce individuals as well as formations.

Background currently limited; treat anything more specific as unconfirmed.

United States

Samuels

active

Hero · United States

A United States hero miniature released in the 2026 wave, alongside the Grizzly and Linebacker plastics. Samuels belongs to the same generation of American characters as Wells: soldiers who are also, unavoidably, experiments.

Background not yet published in detail at the time of writing.

II · Political figures

F
Franklin D. Roosevelt President of the United States · United States

In this history Roosevelt entered the Los Alamos Rift a dying man and came out renewed, convinced he had been given a commission. His January 1945 broadcast reframes the war as a crusade against the Axis and the Soviet Bloc alike — the moment the grand alliance dies and the Pan-Atlantic Alliance is born.

F
Emperor Hirohito Sovereign of Japan · Japan

His July 1945 address acknowledges the destruction visited on Japan and refuses surrender, promising to answer "unprecedented weapons" with Japanese ingenuity. That promise becomes the Iwo Jima laboratories and, ultimately, the Ghost and Shibito programmes.

F
August von Mettenbach Neu Republik Minister · Axis

The civilian voice of the Neu Republik, and the source of the setting’s most quoted line about the fires of Dresden. Von Mettenbach frames the American attack not as a catastrophe but as an inheritance: the Allies opened the door, and the Vault walked through it.

F
Mikhail Gurin Soviet Commissar · Soviet Bloc

A commissar’s voice from the Science Cities: devoted, implacable, and entirely clear about the intended fate of anyone who resists. Gurin’s published line — "they will be re-educated, or they will be dead" — is the Soviet Bloc’s position on the Rift, on Europe, and on its own soldiers.

F
Charles de Gaulle Leader of Free France · United States

Liberates Paris in August 1944, roughly on the historic schedule, and inherits a country whose countryside is being patrolled by the reanimated. Free France’s position in the Pan-Atlantic Alliance is defined by that contradiction: a liberated capital, and a nation that cannot safely travel between its own towns.

Where a named character’s background has not been published in detail, the dossier says so. The archive does not write fiction to fill a gap: an absence in the record is itself part of the record.
