Chronology · 1943–1947

The Broken Years

Twenty-six dated entries, ordered as the record orders them. Each is graded: canon where the published lore states it, implied where it is strongly suggested, contested where the community reads more into the sources than the sources say.

1943

1 entries

A conspiracy older than the Reich continues to steer Germany from behind its institutions.

The Green Vault had spent centuries embedded in European power — secret societies, friendly ministries, agents placed even inside the American OSS. It treated the Nazi regime as an instrument rather than an ally, useful for as long as it delivered a Germany capable of being aimed. Nothing in this period looks different from our own history. That is the point.

Sources: TV Tropes · Tabletop Battles

1944

7 entries

American atomic testing at Los Alamos does not merely succeed. It opens something.

The Trinity test is the hinge on which the whole setting turns. The detonation tears a rift in space and time above the New Mexico desert — a wound that does not close, and through which something other than light comes back. Every later technology in the war is downstream of this moment: the Rift-tech weapons, the walkers, the serum, the dead who keep walking.

Sources: Chicago Dice · Tabletop Battles · Warlord Games

Within a month of Trinity, an atomic bomb destroys Dresden. A second Rift opens at its centre.

The Allies answer the Los Alamos event by demonstrating that they alone control it. Dresden is erased. Tens of thousands die, and in the crater a second Rift opens — a permanent distortion in the middle of a ruined German city, where physics behaves like a rumour. The city becomes the most valuable and most dangerous square kilometre on Earth.

Sources: Tabletop Battles · Warlord Games · Chicago Dice

The cabal abandons secrecy, purges the Nazi leadership and founds the Neu Republik.

With a Rift of their own and a nation in shock, the Green Vault judged that the Nazis had served their purpose. The regime is removed from the inside — not conquered, replaced — and a new order is declared: the Neu Republik, equally authoritarian, considerably more competent, and entirely willing to use what came through the Dresden wound. Axis forces begin to field Rift-tech months before any Allied equivalent.

Sources: Tabletop Battles · TV Tropes

Dresden’s dead are conscripted. The Totenkorps becomes an industrial process.

Oberstarzt Albrecht von Zögling — "Der Erntemann", the Harvestman — turns the Rift’s residue into a manufacturing input. The Totenkorps are not a handful of resurrected soldiers but a production line: refrigerated transport, serum injection, and a horde that does not need feeding, rest, or convincing. German front-line officers are divided on the programme. The programme does not care.

Sources: OnTableTop · Touche Critique

The Normandy landings go in against undead shock regiments instead of a broken garrison.

Operation Overlord proceeds on the historic timetable and meets a battlefield that history does not recognise. Totenkorps regiments are committed against the Allied beachheads as shock troops: slow, unbreakable, and horrifying in a way that no amount of training addresses. The landings succeed in places, stall in others, and never produce the breakout the planners promised.

Sources: Tabletop Battles · Touche Critique

De Gaulle enters Paris. Outside the city, the dead are still walking the fields.

The liberation of Paris happens roughly on schedule and is, for a few weeks, the last thing in Europe that resembles the war everyone expected. The resistance networks that made it possible are already reporting something else: the French countryside is being patrolled by the reanimated. The war has acquired a second population.

Sources: Touche Critique

A dying Roosevelt enters the Los Alamos Rift and returns restored, certain he has heard God.

The exact sequence is deliberately vague in the sources: a President in failing health, a visit to the site, an entry into the Rift, and a return. What is not vague is the consequence. Roosevelt emerges physically renewed and publicly certain that he has been given a commission. American war aims are rewritten in the language of a crusade almost overnight.

Sources: Tabletop Battles · Warlord Games

1945

7 entries

Roosevelt reframes the war as a crusade against fascism and godless Communism alike.

In a broadcast that the Field Manual preserves, Roosevelt names both enemy blocs in the same breath — the "hell-spawned abominations of the Axis and Soviet regimes". The Soviet alliance, already strained by the Rift question, is ideologically finished. Britain and the surviving European governments stay in the American camp as the Pan-Atlantic Alliance, but privately, and increasingly publicly, resent a partner that will not share its Rift technology.

Sources: Warlord Games · TV Tropes

Germany’s serum programme produces its first werewolf shock troops, and they work.

The soldier-enhancement division at Dresden had been injecting volunteers with a serum built from reptile, bat and alligator material. The first variant to reach the front is the Schreckwulfen: bestial, fast, and devastating against infantry. Two further variants follow — the Nachtjäger, more monstrous, and the Nachtalben, which look uncomfortably like the vampire of folklore. Allied infantry formations begin requesting heavier support as a standing item.

Sources: TV Tropes · Warlord Community

Soviet genetic programmes field the Ursus: eight feet of man and bear.

The Soviet Bloc could not match the Axis serum, so it built a different answer to the same problem: heavy infantry that could survive contact with a Schreckwulfen. The Ursus is the flagship of that effort, and the beginning of a broader programme that will produce the Moroz Gul and the Daughters of the Motherland. Soviet Rift science acquires its character here — brutal, improvised, and aimed squarely at the human body.

Sources: Warlord Games · TV Tropes

Japan stabilises its own Rift programme on Iwo Jima and changes the Pacific war.

Hirohito’s address acknowledges both the destruction visited on Japan and the determination to answer it with "innovations of our own ingenuity". Japanese Rift-tech is the least explained and most unsettling in the setting: compression weapons that fold a target in on itself, and infantry who phase out of the physical world. The programme is supported, in part, by Axis knowledge — a debt whose terms are never stated publicly.

Sources: Warlord Games · TV Tropes

The atomic strikes on Japan do not end the war. The Shibito programme begins filling the gaps.

In our history these are the last days of the war. In this one they are a phase change. Japan does not surrender; it industrialises its dead instead. The Shibito squads — undead soldiers immune to fear and utterly obedient — become the Imperial answer to attrition. Community reading of the sources holds that the Green Vault had a hand in the targeting of the strikes, and that the Vault’s interests, not Allied strategy, decided where the bombs fell. Warlord has not confirmed it.

Sources: TV Tropes · Warlord Games

The Western allies formalise a partnership built on shared enemies and unshared secrets.

Britain, the Commonwealth and the surviving European governments align formally with the United States. The alliance holds because the alternative is the Neu Republik, not because its members trust each other. The central grievance is technology: American Rift work is shared on a licensing basis, in American-built hulls, under American terms. Commonwealth automation is the British answer to that dependency — build your own miracles, in your own factories.

Sources: TV Tropes · Warlord Games

The historical end date passes with the Reich still fighting, and better equipped than its enemies.

May 1945 comes and goes. The Neu Republik is not collapsing; it is manufacturing. With the Dresden Rift feeding its laboratories and a labour force drawn from occupied Europe, the Axis holds a technological advantage that the Allies can only close by capturing examples — which is precisely what the Rift War becomes about.

Sources: Tabletop Battles · Battle Leader

1946

3 entries

Three blocs, four Rifts, no decisive advantage. Europe becomes an arms race with a front line.

The war settles into something worse than a stalemate: an equilibrium in which every power can build weapons the others cannot counter, and none can build enough of them. Walkers multiply. Tesla weapons become standard issue on both sides of the Atlantic alliance. The Soviet Bloc loots and adapts. Japan fortifies the Pacific. The Neu Republik fortifies the Rift.

Sources: Battle Leader · Chicago Dice

Commonwealth industry begins replacing infantry sections with machines that do not flinch.

British computing research crosses a threshold, and the Commonwealth begins fielding formed units of automated infantry under automated directors. The implications are noticed at home as well as at the front: automatons appear in civilian work, in pubs, in the dockyards. One soldier’s recorded reaction — going back to the front rather than stay in a town where machines had taken every job — is among the bleakest jokes in the setting.

Sources: Warlord Community · TV Tropes

The Allies convene to decide what, legally and morally, the dead are.

Festung Europa opens this file without resolving it. The conference is an attempt by the Pan-Atlantic Alliance to establish a common position on captured Totenkorps, on the status of the reanimated as prisoners, and on whether the process can be reversed. The Axis does not attend. The document survives as evidence that the Allies understood exactly what they were looking at, and chose to keep fighting anyway.

Sources: Touche Critique

1947

7 entries

The first Allied push of the Rift War year opens in the west.

Pluto is one of three operations detailed in Festung Europa, and the earliest — a winter-to-summer offensive intended to test whether the Neu Republik’s line can still be moved by conventional mass. It cannot, decisively. The operation produces ground, casualties, and a great deal of intelligence about what the Axis has been building behind the Westwall.

Sources: Touche Critique

A second offensive opens in February and grinds for five months.

Valentine overlaps Pluto and runs twice as long. Festung Europa presents it as a campaign of attrition fought for a specific objective — the approaches to the Dresden Rift — and structures it as a playable narrative: a chronology, a hero, a grand scenario, and a set of linked engagements.

Sources: Touche Critique

The Rift War series opens in Normandy: the Allies still cannot break the Axis, and Dresden is the prize.

Warlord frames the supplement’s starting position plainly. Since Dresden opened, "new and unspeakable horrors have poured forth from the heart of the newly emergent Neu Republik". The Allied plan to finish the war in Europe is in ruins, and all sides now deploy weapons of unprecedented destructiveness with increasing frequency, leaving Europe in ruin and nobody able to gain the upper hand. This is the state of the world the archive describes.

Sources: OnTableTop · Touche Critique

The Allies go into Germany itself, with Roberta Wells and the 109th Airborne leading.

Touchdown is the deepest Allied thrust of the year and the campaign Warlord chose to hang its launch miniature on: Roberta Wells, in prototype Firefly armour, flying in with the 109th Airborne Division. The operation runs into September. Its opponent is not only the Neu Republik’s field army but the Totenkorps infrastructure that von Zögling has spent three years building.

Sources: OnTableTop · Touche Critique

The offensive resumes with the Axis line bent but not broken.

The second phase of Valentine runs into October, overlapping Touchdown and the festival of horrors that the Dresden approaches have become. The supplement gives it the same architecture as its siblings: a chronology to play through, a named hero at the centre, and a grand scenario to close it.

Sources: Touche Critique

The final chapter of the first volume: the Rift city, and everyone who wants it.

Festung Europa closes on Dresden itself — the wound, the city built around it, and the Neu Republik’s determination that no one else will ever own it. In the current state of the narrative, nobody does. The war ends the volume exactly where it began: an unreachable objective, three alliances bleeding for it, and a Rift that is still open.

Sources: Touche Critique · Warlord Community

The second Rift War volume turns to the Pacific and the Japanese threat to the American mainland.

With Festung Europa establishing the European theatre, the series turns outward. Invasion Amerika — volume two — shifts the narrative to the Pacific and to Japan’s ambitions against the American continent, with the Empire of Japan finally taking a leading role. A third volume was on pre-order by late 2026. This archive tracks the published state of the lore and marks anything beyond it as speculation.

Sources: Warlord Community · Warlord Games

Dates given as a month or year are as published. Where a source describes an event in retrospect — the President’s renewal, the Pan-Atlantic Alliance, the Green Vault’s role in the Pacific — the archive says so rather than inventing a day.
