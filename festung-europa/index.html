Rift War · Volume One · Supplement

Festung
Europa

One hundred and sixteen pages that turn a setting into a history. The European theatre from February 1944 to February 1947, three offensives, one conference, and a city nobody can hold.

The book opens in April 1947, in Normandy, with a German officer watching an American raid come in and discovering that the world he trained for has been replaced. That is the correct entry point: Festung Europa is not a list of new units, it is the argument that the war did not end and cannot be ended by the methods that were supposed to end it.

Warlord’s own synopsis sets the position without flinching. Since the Dresden Rift opened, “new and unspeakable horrors have poured forth from the heart of the newly emergent Neu Republik”. The Allied plan for Europe is in tatters, every belligerent is deploying weapons of unprecedented destructive capacity, and no one can gain the upper hand.

Structurally the volume is a campaign book, not a rulebook: history first, then three operations presented as linked scenarios, each with a chronology, a named hero at its centre and a grand scenario to close it, plus a final chapter on Dresden itself.

The 1947 offensive

Four operations,
one objective

All dates as published. Bars run February to October 1947.

Operation Pluto

12 Feb — 14 Jun 1947

The first Allied offensive of the year, and the test of whether the Neu Republik’s line can still be moved by conventional mass. It cannot — not decisively. Pluto buys ground, casualties and intelligence, in that order of satisfaction.

United StatesBritish CommonwealthAxis

Operation Valentine · I

14 Feb — 10 Jul 1947

A second, longer offensive running alongside Pluto, aimed at the approaches to the Dresden Rift. Presented in the book as a linked campaign: chronology, hero, grand scenario, engagements.

Pan-AtlanticSoviet BlocAxis

Operation Touchdown

23 Jun — 6 Sep 1947

The deepest thrust into Germany, led by Roberta Wells and the 109th Airborne Division. Its real opponent is not the Neu Republik’s field army but the Totenkorps infrastructure behind it.

United StatesBritish CommonwealthAxis

Operation Valentine · II

30 Jul — 10 Oct 1947

The offensive resumes against a line bent but unbroken, and overlaps Touchdown. By October the Allies hold more of Germany than they did in February, and none of Dresden.

Cast

Two people,
one wound

The volume is built around a pair of opposed characters, and Warlord released both alongside the book.

United States · Firefly

Roberta Wells

America’s leading Rift researcher, attached to the 109th Airborne Division in prototype armour. The book’s Operation Touchdown miniature is her, landing in Germany.

Axis · Totenkorps

Oberstarzt Albrecht von Zögling

“Der Erntemann” — the Harvestman. Runs the necromobilization programme, leads his Totenrotte personally, and carries medical equipment nobody wants to be injected with.
