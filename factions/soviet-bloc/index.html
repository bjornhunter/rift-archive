SB-04 · Belligerent record

The Soviet Bloc

Science Cities · The Long War

Mass infantryGeneticsSonic weaponsArtillery

Insignia · reconstruction

The Soviet Bloc never had a Rift of its own. It had something arguably more dangerous: a state apparatus willing to reverse-engineer the future from salvage, and a population it was prepared to spend without limit.

Soviet Rift science went where the others would not — into the body. The Science Cities produced the Ursus, eight feet of man and bear built to trade blows with Schreckwulfen; the Moroz Gul, terror troops shaped by cold; and the Daughters of the Motherland, an elite of enhanced women whose invincibility is partly manufactured by deploying them where they will not be filmed losing.

Soviet doctrine is mass with teeth: waves of cheap, well-equipped infantry, commissars behind them, artillery and stolen walkers in support. They do not outfight you. They outlast you.

Relationship to the Rift

Salvage and surgery. Soviet Rift-tech is crude, copied and often bolted onto a chassis that was already obsolete — and it works, because the Union measures success in ground held, not elegance.

Battlefield role

Horde. Large resilient infantry formations backed by heavy artillery, tanks and looted walkers.

Signature methods

Ursus heavy infantry, Moroz Gul terror troops, Zvukovoy sonic weapons that shake targets apart, commissars and “Not one step back”.

Cost

Slow and attritional. Soviet commanders win by making the enemy’s best units irrelevant through sheer number of problems.
